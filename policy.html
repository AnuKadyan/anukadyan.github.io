Policy analysis on geothermal finance, EV industrial strategy, climate finance and development, written at Columbia SIPA.

SIPA CEE Practicum · 2026 · Data Analysis Lead · with J. Vesey, M. Howland, A. Korcova · Faculty advisor: Prof. Goksenin Ozturkeri · Funded by CGEP and the Columbia Climate School

Beneath the Subsurface: Unlocking Hawaiʻi's Geothermal Potential

A financial model of a 38 MW geothermal project (12.6% unlevered IRR) across seven policy scenarios. A $45–60M state exploration program would cut the dry-well rate from 68% to 24%, and a BLM-style tiered royalty would lower the required PPA price to $57/MWh with no new public spending. Includes fieldwork on Oʻahu and Hawaiʻi Island with HSEO, UH Mānoa, state legislators and Puna Geothermal Venture.

Global Energy Policy · December 2025 · with J. Hochleitner, F. Lorenz

The EU's Industrial Policy in the EV Sector: Learning from China's Integrated EV and Critical Minerals Strategy

Traces three decades of Chinese NEV and battery policy against the EU's fragmented toolkit, and argues for EU-level demand-side support, battery production as a core policy goal, and long-term finance for critical-mineral exploration.

Course memo · Financing and Investing in Sustainable Development (Prof. Aniket Shah) · March 2026

Climate-Aligned Performance Benchmarks for Sovereign Borrowers

Proposes that the World Bank link concessional loan pricing to co-designed climate benchmarks, piloted with five to seven Sub-Saharan African borrowers, to close the accountability gap and cut the policy-risk premium on clean energy finance.

Final policy memo · Quantitative Analysis II · May 2026

Does Microfinance Reduce Poverty?

Weighs evidence from India, Vietnam and Bangladesh: microcredit brings short-term gains in borrowing and small-business activity but no robust, lasting reduction in poverty, so it should be targeted and rigorously evaluated rather than scaled as a standalone tool.
